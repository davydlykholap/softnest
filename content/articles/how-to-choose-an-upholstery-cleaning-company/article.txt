Will the price change when the cleaner arrives? Will they give the stained armrests enough attention? How will they protect your furniture and the floor around it?

When choosing an upholstery cleaning company, look for a clear written quote, a cleaning method suited to your fabric, and straightforward answers about stain treatment, drying and follow-up support.

Whether you’re hiring a cleaner in Mississauga or elsewhere in the GTA, these five checks will help you compare services and understand what you’re paying for.

1. Check What the Upholstery Cleaning Price Includes

Two companies may both offer “sofa cleaning” while including different amounts of work. Before comparing prices, find out what each quote covers.

A clear quote should explain:

Which parts of the furniture will be cleaned, including the seats, back, armrests, sides and both sides of removable cushions where suitable.

Whether treatment of common household stains and heavily used areas is included.

Whether extra time or repeat treatment could increase the price.

Whether tax, travel charges or parking costs are included or separate.

Suppose you send photos showing dirty armrests and receive a quote. Ask: “If those areas need extra attention or another treatment, will the price stay the same?”

Adding an armchair to the booking changes the job. Spending more time on an armrest you already showed the company is a different situation. You should understand how each would affect the price before cleaning begins. Specialized work, such as treating pet urine inside the padding, may also be quoted separately; ask about it when describing the problem.

Included stain treatment does not mean every mark is guaranteed to disappear. It means the cleaner will assess the stains and use an appropriate treatment. The result depends on the stain, the fabric and any previous attempts to remove it.

Get the service and price confirmed in writing. If the price is an estimate, ask what could change it. Any change should be explained and agreed upon before work starts.

2. Ask How They Assess the Fabric Before Cleaning

A careful approach starts with questions about your furniture: its size, seat count, condition and the stains you want addressed. Photos can help the company understand the job. At SoftNest, they are optional; you can describe the furniture by phone or message instead.

The cleaner should also ask about previous cleaning attempts. If you have used a spot remover or homemade solution, tell them what you applied. A photo of the product label can help.

Before starting, the technician should inspect the upholstery, seams and areas of concern, consider the manufacturer’s care information, and test an inconspicuous area when needed. The cleaning method should suit the fabric and its condition.

A useful explanation is specific to your furniture. The technician might point out dirt from everyday use that is likely to come out, a stain that needs special attention, or an area where the fabric has faded. If they mention training or certification, ask who issued it and whether it covers upholstery cleaning.

An assessment should explain how the material and condition affect the result. Most suitable furniture and everyday marks can be cleaned; old stains, fading or worn areas can also be assessed for possible improvement. Complete removal or restoration should not be promised before that assessment.

3. Find Out How They Protect Your Home and Treat Heavily Used Areas

Professional upholstery cleaning also involves taking care of the space around the furniture.

Ask how the company protects nearby floors, rugs and furnishings from moisture and cleaning products. Where liquid could reach a wood floor, for example, the protective covering needs to prevent it from soaking through.

Hoses and equipment should be positioned carefully around corners, furniture and walkways. Photos of actual cleaning jobs can show these details more clearly than close-up before-and-after pictures alone.

The cleaning should also account for how the furniture is used. Favourite seats and armrests often need more attention than other sections. That may involve pretreatment, time for the product to work, and repeat treatment where needed.

Two or three passes with a cleaning tool are not automatically better than one. What matters is whether the technician checks the result and gives an area more attention when needed, as long as further cleaning is safe for the fabric.

Ask whether this work is included in the quoted service. You should know whether the price covers treatment of the visible dirt and stains you want addressed.

For a condo appointment, also confirm parking, elevator access and any building requirements in advance. This helps the company plan enough time and explain any related costs before the visit.

4. Ask About Rinsing and Drying

If the company uses hot water extraction, often called steam cleaning, ask how it rinses the upholstery and removes excess moisture.

Applying a cleaning product is only part of the process. Loosened dirt and cleaning solution also need to be extracted. Thorough rinsing helps remove detergent residue that could leave the fabric feeling sticky or soapy.

Rinsing can help remove residues from products previously used at home. A cleaner can assess any resulting marks or changes and try to improve their appearance, without promising restoration. Where appropriate, a suitable rinse helps neutralize alkaline residues; the choice depends on the fabric and products involved.

A useful question is: “How do you rinse out the cleaning products?” An explanation of the process tells you more than a general claim about using eco-friendly products.

Ask about drying before the appointment, too. Drying time depends on the fabric, padding, how much moisture is extracted, room temperature, humidity and airflow. Air movers help speed up drying, but the same drying time will not apply to every sofa and every home.

Before the technician leaves, you should know when you can use the furniture, how to position removable cushions and how to help it dry. You should also know whom to contact if it stays unusually damp beyond the estimated time.

5. Read Detailed Reviews and Check the Service Guarantee

A star rating is a useful starting point. Detailed reviews give you a clearer picture of the service.

Look for comments that address your concerns:

Did the final price match the quote?

Was the cleaner careful with the furniture and the home?

Was the customer happy with the upholstery after it dried?

Did the company respond to questions after the visit?

Reviews involving furniture similar to yours can be especially useful.

Read the company’s responses to complaints as well. One review rarely tells the whole story, but the response can show whether the business listens and offers a practical next step.

A service guarantee should have clear terms. Ask how soon you should report a concern, how it will be assessed, and when a return visit or repeat treatment is available at no extra cost. You can also ask whether the company carries liability insurance that covers the cleaning work being offered. Insurance and a satisfaction guarantee address different concerns.

For example, if a mark becomes visible again after drying, you should know how to contact the company and what happens next. A clear follow-up process gives a guarantee practical value.

Questions to Ask Before Hiring an Upholstery Cleaner

You can send this short message when requesting a quote:

Could you confirm what the quote includes, whether treatment of the stains I described is covered, and whether extra cleaning time could change the price? What would the total price be, including tax and any fees?

A helpful reply will describe the work and state the price, or explain what is needed to confirm it. “Don’t worry, we’ll take care of everything” may sound reassuring, but it leaves you with little information to compare.

Before booking, make sure you also understand how your home will be protected, what to expect during drying and how to contact the company afterward.
