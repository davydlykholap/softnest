# Customer validation for city-service pages

Prepared 2026-10-17. No customer sessions have been conducted yet. Automated checks establish technical behaviour, not customer satisfaction, conversion uplift or search ranking.

## Why this design is a reasonable starting point

- Morkes and Nielsen's 1997 controlled study with 51 participants found higher subjective satisfaction with concise, scannable and factual writing. Its reported 124% improvement was a combined usability score, not a sales or satisfaction uplift. The study concerned travel information: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/
- NN/G's 2020 synthesis of eye-tracking research supports descriptive headings, plain language, bullets and important information early: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-people-read-online/
- Unbounce's 2024 commercial/professional-services benchmark identified a 275–745-word range with stronger conversions in its dataset. This is an observational benchmark across service categories, not a cleaning-specific optimum or a Google requirement: https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/professional-services-conversion-rate/
- Longer pages can also perform well where additional explanations and proof help buyers decide. The Moz case changed several elements together, so its reported uplift cannot be attributed to word count alone: https://conversion-rate-experts.com/moz-case-study/

The working editorial target is roughly 300–450 useful words, with essential information visible and supporting answers expandable. Shorter or longer copy is acceptable when customer needs justify it. No formal reading-grade score or customer satisfaction result has been established for this site.

## Small first round of customer sessions

The business can invite a small first round of prospective customers who resemble its intended audience. Five is a practical starting group for finding problems, not a representative sample or a statistical proof. Include mobile users and someone unfamiliar with SoftNest. Do not use team members as a substitute for prospective customers.

Give each person a scenario rather than instructions naming the controls. For example:

> Your sofa in Oakville needs cleaning after everyday use and a spill. You are comparing providers and want to understand the service and get a price. Use this page as you normally would. Tell us what you are thinking.

Use the built preview or a deployed page. Ask participants to stop before submitting a real request; no real name, phone number or address is needed for this exercise.

Observe without explaining the page:

1. Can they identify the service and city?
2. Can they explain what is included and what could affect the price?
3. Can they find drying expectations and understand the limits on stain removal?
4. Can they find genuine reviews if they want reassurance?
5. Can they start a quote and see the right city and item category? Can they change it?

Record whether they succeed unaided, where they hesitate, anything they misunderstand, and optional task time. After the task ask:

- How easy or difficult was that, from 1 (very difficult) to 5 (very easy)?
- How confident would you feel requesting a quote, from 1 (not confident) to 5 (very confident)?
- What information did you expect but could not find?
- Was anything unnecessary or confusing?

These are simple diagnostic questions, not a validated satisfaction index. Keep anonymous notes and report counts rather than generalizing percentages from a tiny group. Repeated misunderstandings are reasons to revise the copy and repeat a small round.

## After deployment

Check Search Console indexing and search visibility separately from enquiries. Existing GA4 quote clicks, quote starts and successful form submissions can identify funnel friction; a phone-link click does not prove a completed call or booking. Compare device types and traffic sources before attributing a change to page wording. Use actual qualified enquiries and booked work as business outcomes where records are available.

If traffic supports a properly sized experiment, compare a specific copy/layout change against the current version while keeping the offer and acquisition conditions consistent. Choose the outcome and analysis plan beforehand. Do not declare a winner after a handful of leads or compare unrelated cities as if their audiences were interchangeable.
